# How to test supplements on yourself: find out what actually makes a difference for you

Magnesium, ashwagandha, creatine – online you can read about everything each supplement supposedly does.
But what other people report doesn't have to be true for you. The good news: you can try it yourself – in a
structured way instead of by gut feeling. This guide shows you how.

> Important: a self-experiment doesn't replace a study or medical advice. If you have health issues, are
> pregnant or breastfeeding, or take medication, talk to your doctor or pharmacist first.

## Why gut feeling so often fools you

- **Several things at once:** Start three new supplements together and you'll never know which one did what – or whether it was just a calm week.
- **Expectation:** When you try something new and hope it helps, you often feel better for that reason alone (the placebo effect).
- **Regression to the mean:** Many people start a supplement exactly when they feel worst. Afterwards things often improve on their own – and the supplement gets the credit.
- **Memory:** "I slept better last week" is hardly reliable without notes.

## The 5-step method

### 1. Know your normal (baseline)

Take a few days (ideally 5–7) **without anything new** and rate how you felt each evening: sleep, energy,
calm, focus – for example on a scale from 1 to 5. That's your reference. Without it, you can't tell later
whether anything changed at all.

### 2. One at a time

Only ever test **one** supplement at a time. Everything you've been taking for a while stays exactly the same.
That way you can attribute changes to one thing.

### 3. Same conditions

Take the supplement at roughly the **same time** in the **same amount** every day. Follow the recommended
intake on the package. Note special days (alcohol, little sleep, illness, travel, workouts) – these are
confounders that can skew the result.

### 4. Rate it every evening – one minute

Rate the same areas as in your baseline. Short and honest – don't overthink it. One minute per evening is
enough. What matters is doing it **every day**.

### 5. Compare – and take a break

After about 1–2 weeks (longer for some supplements), compare your test days with your baseline. A good trick:
**stop the supplement for a few days afterwards.** If your ratings drop back to your normal, that points more
toward a real difference for you. If they stay the same, it may have been something else.

## How long should a test take?

It depends on how quickly anything can be expected at all, according to manufacturers and research. Some
things people notice within days, for others several weeks are mentioned. Rule of thumb: test a bit longer,
and one thing at a time.

## What a self-experiment can – and can't – do

- ✅ It shows you how **you feel** with and without a supplement – in your everyday life.
- ✅ It helps you save money: whatever doesn't make a noticeable difference, you can drop.
- ❌ It doesn't prove that a supplement "works" – that would take blinded studies with many people.
- ❌ It says nothing about lab values (e.g. vitamin D levels) – only a blood test measures those.

**Tip for advanced testers:** For more rigor, have a second person blind you: on some days they fill the
supplement, on others an identical-looking placebo into a container – and only reveal at the end which day
was which.
